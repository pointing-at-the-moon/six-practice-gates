## Abstract
This paper closes Part VII of the NIAN series by examining Samantabhadra's eighth vow (*constantly following the Buddha in study*), ninth vow (*accommodating sentient beings*), and tenth vow (*universal dedication*). T0293 shows that their closure clauses follow the same formula used by the preceding seven vows, while vow ten explicitly gathers the merits “from the initial reverencing through to the accommodating.” At the commentarial level, Zongmi aligns vows eight, nine, and ten with dedication to bodhi, dedication to sentient beings, and the dedication that comprises all three locations. Chengguan, while explaining vow ten's three directions of dedication, states: “toward the absolute, relying on substance; toward bodhi, relying on attribute; toward sentient beings, relying on function.” These sources support the local structure of the three directions and vows eight through ten; neither commentator maps vows one through seven onto substance, attribute, and function. Within that limit, Shi Huijing proposes an **authorial heuristic** that retrospectively reads vows one through three as *substance*, vows four through nine as *attribute*, and vow ten as *function*. This whole-ten-vow mapping synthesises the arguments of NIAN P26–P28; it is not a proposition of T0293 and not an inherited teaching of Chengguan or Zongmi. Its purpose is to offer a testable reading aid, not to establish a new canonical classification. On that basis, the paper places a practice- and ritual-oriented interpretation beside Francis H. Cook's metaphysics-of-totality reading, with Paul Williams and Imre Hamar serving as comparative interlocutors.

**Keywords:** Samantabhadra's vow-practice; *Bhadracarī-praṇidhāna*; constantly following the Buddha in study; accommodating sentient beings; universal dedication; vows eight through ten; three-direction dedication; substance, attribute, and function; authorial synthesis; heuristic mapping; hermeneutical boundary; closure-formula isomorphism; great-tree-king simile; *pariṇāmana*; Chengguan; Zongmi; *Practice-and-Vow Chapter*; metaphysics of totality; jewel-net of Indra.

## I. Introduction

NIAN P26 read vows one through three as a three-karma assembly; P27 read vows four and five as a karma dyad; P28 read vows six and seven as two modes of requesting the dharma's persistence. Those divisions are the series author's analyses, not divisions marked by T0293 itself. The present paper turns to vows eight through ten. Zongmi explicitly aligns them with dedication to bodhi, dedication to sentient beings, and the dedication that comprises all three locations. Chengguan explains the three directions as relying respectively on attribute, function, and substance. This local threefold structure, unlike the later whole-ten-vow mapping proposed in this paper, has direct commentarial support.

Two opposed misreadings dominate the Anglophone reception of vow ten and require correction at the outset. The first reduces *universal dedication* to a kind of **merit-as-economic-transaction** — meritorious conduct treated as a transferable commodity, the verb *to dedicate* (*pariṇāmana*) drained into a mercantile gesture of moving merit from one ledger to another. This misreading collapses the cosmological-ritual articulation of *turning-toward* into a transaction. The second reduces *universal dedication* to **purely interior mindful presencing** — the modernist-psychologising pole — in which the entire vow-king becomes an inner exercise upon the present moment of awareness, the *universal* dimension drained altogether. This misreading collapses the outward cosmological breadth into inward-only attention. The Chinese-patriarchal line *"Samantabhadra's vow-king is not apart from the present moment of mind"* (Yunqi Zhuhong's *Sub-Commentary on the Amitābha Sūtra*, X22n0424) does not advance the second misreading; it teaches that the *outward universal architecture* of vow ten is simultaneously *condensed-and-gathered* within the present moment of buddha-recitation — the outer is contained-in-the-inner, the inner manifesting the outer, and neither reducible to the other.

The thesis therefore has two distinct levels. At the source-verifiable level, all ten vows share the same closure pattern, vow ten explicitly gathers the merits of the preceding nine, and the Tang commentaries explain the three directions of dedication and their relation to vows eight through ten. At the authorial level, Shi Huijing retrospectively maps vows 1–3 to *substance*, vows 4–9 to *attribute*, and vow 10 to *function* in order to synthesise NIAN P26–P28. The latter is a modern heuristic proposal. Neither T0293 nor the two commentaries assigns vows one through seven to that schema, and they must not be cited as ancient ratification of the whole-ten-vow map.[^24]

## II. Textual Sources and Recensional Configuration

The principal textual base is T0293 *Mahāvaipulya-buddhāvataṃsaka-sūtra: The Practice-and-Vow Chapter of Samantabhadra Entering the Inconceivable Liberation Realm*, fascicle forty, translated by Prajña (般若, c. 734 – c. 810) at Chang'an in 798 CE. One philological observation must be marked at the outset because it bears on the entire interpretive configuration: **T0278 (the sixty-fascicle *Avataṃsaka*, translated by Buddhabhadra in 421 CE) and T0279 (the eighty-fascicle *Avataṃsaka*, translated by Śikṣānanda 695–699 CE) both lack the *Practice-and-Vow Chapter*.** Only T0293 (the Prajña recension, completed in 798 CE) contains the prose ten-vow ladder. Chengguan attests this fact himself in the opening pages of his *Practice-and-Vow Chapter Commentary*: *"this chapter is not present in the principal scripture; in the Zhenyuan period a Sanskrit manuscript was obtained from Kashmir, and Prajña Tripiṭaka therefore translated it and inserted it into the present sūtra."*[^3] The complete ten-vow sequence in the extant Chinese recensions therefore appears in the late-eighth-century T0293 rather than in T0278 or T0279. This paper's whole-ten-vow substance-attribute-function mapping cannot be described as an early Huayan consensus—or as a late-Tang commentarial doctrine. It is a present-day authorial synthesis built beside, but extending beyond, the narrower Chengguan–Zongmi evidence.

Two Tang commentaries supply the local interpretive evidence. In his discussion of vow ten's dedication of one's cultivated good toward three locations, Chengguan states: *"toward the absolute, relying on substance; toward bodhi, relying on attribute; toward sentient beings, relying on function,"* and adds *"transforming below with great compassion, seeking above with great wisdom."* Zongmi explicitly aligns vows eight, nine, and ten with dedication to bodhi, dedication to sentient beings, and the dedication that comprises all three locations. Together they support the three directions and the local vows-eight-through-ten structure. They do not assign vows one through seven to substance, attribute, and function; whenever this paper extends the schema across all ten vows, that move belongs to Shi Huijing's heuristic synthesis.

The doctrinal-treatise layer is supplied by five texts. T1666 *Awakening of Faith in the Mahāyāna* supplies the canonical articulation of the three greats — *substance / attribute / function* — in its opening fascicle's *meaning-establishing section*, the locus classicus of the schema (translation traditionally attributed to Aśvaghoṣa rendered by Paramārtha; the modern philological dispute over Indian-original-versus-late-Tang-Chinese-composition is held to a single footnote and not developed[^4]). T1509 *Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra* fascicle sixty-one *Rejoicing-and-Dedication* chapter contributes the *three-mode reverence* (repentance, rejoicing-and-dedication, exhortation-request) and the *three marks* (designation-mark, dharma-mark, signless-mark) that supply the Indic-treatise root for the dedication schema. T1593 *Mahāyāna-saṃgraha* fascicle three identifies the **fifth of the six supremacies** as *dedication-supremacy* (*pariṇāmana-supremacy*) — the canonical-treatise placement of dedication within the perfection scheme. T1564 *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* and T1585 *Cheng weishi lun* — both worked out at length in NIAN P28 — are referred to here only by single-line cross-reference. The *Ten-Dedications Chapter* in T0278 / T0279 is a separate textual tradition and, while doctrinally adjacent, is not developed in the present paper.

The Chinese ritual-practice layer is supplied by the convergence of three traditions. Zhiyi's *Lotus Samādhi Repentance Liturgy* (T1941) places **dedication-and-vow** within the **seventh** of its ten ritual sections (the corrected placement—not the tenth section—as the original *kepan* division confirms[^5]). Shandao's *Verses for the Six Times of Reverence-Praise* (T1980) places dedication as the **fifth** of its five mind-gates. Yunqi Zhuhong's *Sub-Commentary on the Amitābha Sūtra* (X22n0424) supplies the most concentrated Chinese-patriarchal articulation: *"Samantabhadra's vow-king is not apart from the present moment of mind."* Ouyi Zhixu's *Essentials of the Amitābha Sūtra* (T1762) gathers Samantabhadra's myriad practices into the faith-vow-recitation framework. Yongming Yanshou's *Universal Convergence of the Ten Thousand Goods* (T2017) articulates the substance-function frame for the entire vow-king with the line *"Samantabhadra adorns principle through practice."* The *Three-Time Mindfulness Service* (X74n1464, traditionally attributed to the Zhongfeng line) chants the ten-vow set within a triple recitation framework. These five Chinese-tradition texts together constitute the ritual-operative anchor for the present paper's §III.6.

## III. Core Argument

### 3.1 Closure-Formula Isomorphism and Vow Ten's Explicit Gathering of the First Nine

Each of the ten great vows in T0293 fascicle forty closes with the same four-exhaustion-four-inexhaustion formula. NIAN P26 confirmed this isomorphism for vows one through three; NIAN P27 extended it through vow five; NIAN P28 extended it through vow seven. The present paper extends the empirical verification through vows eight, nine, and ten — the fourth-time confirmation:

| Vow | Operator verb | Closure clause |
|:---:|:---|:---|
| 1 reverencing all buddhas | *reverencing* | *"my reverencing is without end, moment-by-moment continuous, without interruption, body and speech and mind never wearying"* |
| 2 praising the tathāgatas | *praising* | *"my praising is without end ..."* |
| 3 making offerings broadly | *offering* | *"my offering is without end ..."* |
| 4 repenting karmic obstructions | *repenting* | *"my repenting is without end ..."* |
| 5 rejoicing along in others' merits | *rejoicing* | *"my rejoicing is without end ..."* |
| 6 requesting the dharma-wheel be turned | *exhortation-to-turn* | *"my exhortation is without end ..."* |
| 7 requesting the buddhas to abide | *exhortation-to-abide* | *"my exhortation is without end ..."* |
| **8 constantly following the Buddha in study** | ***following-in-study*** | ***"my following-in-study is without end, moment-by-moment continuous, without interruption, body and speech and mind never wearying"*** |
| **9 accommodating sentient beings** | ***accommodating*** | ***"my accommodating is without end, moment-by-moment continuous, without interruption, body and speech and mind never wearying"*** |
| **10 universal dedication** | ***dedicating*** | ***"my dedicating is without end, moment-by-moment continuous, without interruption, body and speech and mind never wearying"*** |

The closure-formula isomorphism holds across vows one through ten at one hundred percent — the only variation is the operator verb (*reverencing / praising / offering / repenting / rejoicing / exhortation-to-turn / exhortation-to-abide / following-in-study / accommodating / dedicating*).[^6]

A philological observation of substantive consequence must be lodged here. **Vow ten's closure structure is itself isomorphic with the closure formulas of vows one through nine; the gathering function of vow ten is not encoded in a distinctive closure.** It appears instead in vow ten's opening sentence: *"as for universal dedication: from the initial reverencing through to the accommodating, all merits whatsoever are entirely dedicated …"*[^7] This sentence explicitly gathers the merits of vows one through nine and is therefore textual evidence that the ten vows are connected. It does not, however, attest the four-part sequence *three-karma assembly → karma dyad → request-structure dyad → dedication triad*. That sequence is the author's retrospective organisation of the material.

### 3.2 Vow Eight: Constantly Following the Buddha in Study — the Upward-Bearing Dimension

Vow eight's principal exposition opens: *"as for constantly following the Buddha in study: of all the tathāgatas in the dust-mote-realms of all the buddha-fields throughout the entire dharmadhātu, throughout space, in the ten directions and three times — of every one of them, I take their practice as my study."*[^8] The object of study is the entire practice-record of **Vairocana as original master** — the bodhisattva's first arousing of the bodhi-mind, the unflagging effort, the flaying of skin to make paper and the drawing of blood to make ink, the writing of scriptures piled high as Sumeru, the sacrifice of head and eyes and limbs without sparing the body, and finally the attainment of great enlightenment beneath the tree.[^9] These narratives — the *body-sacrifice for the dharma* cluster of Indian Mahāyāna scripture — find here their Avataṃsaka articulation, gathered into the single object of *constantly following*. Vow eight's closure: *"as space is exhausted, as the realms of sentient beings are exhausted, as their karma is exhausted, as their afflictions are exhausted, my following-in-study is without end, moment-by-moment continuous, without interruption, body and speech and mind never wearying."*[^10] Vow eight's verse-couplet: *"I follow all the tathāgatas in study, cultivating the perfectly-circular practices of Samantabhadra ..."*[^11]

Vow eight's dimension is **upward-bearing**. Zongmi calls vow eight dedication to bodhi; Chengguan, in his separate account of the three directions, says that dedication toward bodhi relies on attribute. Read together, the two passages support a local relation between vow eight, bodhi, and attribute. Chengguan does not independently present this as a vow-by-vow classification of vow eight. The *Lotus Sūtra*'s *Encouragement of Samantabhadra* chapter (T0262) supplies a one-line cross-reference, but is not developed here.

### 3.3 Vow Nine: Accommodating Sentient Beings — the Downward-Transforming Dimension

Vow nine's principal exposition opens: *"as for accommodating sentient beings: of every kind of sentient being throughout the entire dharmadhātu, throughout space, in the buddha-realms of the ten directions … of every kind, I accommodate and turn, attending in every way, making offerings in every way, as one would revere father and mother, as one would serve teacher and elder, as one would honour an arhat or even the tathāgata, equal and without distinction."*[^12] The line *"equal and without distinction"* is the doctrinal core of vow nine's exposition: in the bodhisattva's accommodation, sentient being and tathāgata are equal in the field of compassionate response.

The decisive image of vow nine is the **great-tree-king simile**:

> *"It is like a great tree-king growing in a desert wilderness of sand and gravel: when the root is watered, every branch and leaf, flower and fruit, flourishes. The bodhi-tree-king of the wilderness of birth-and-death is just so. All sentient beings are its root; all the buddhas and bodhisattvas are its flowers and fruits. When the water of great compassion benefits sentient beings, the wisdom-flowers-and-fruits of the buddhas and bodhisattvas are accomplished."*[^13]

This simile is the **thesis-pivot** of vow nine. It articulates a structural co-dependence among bodhisattva, sentient being, and buddha across the medium of the great-compassion water: sentient beings are the root, buddhas and bodhisattvas are the flowers and fruits, the great-compassion water is the connecting flow. Without the watering of sentient beings by great compassion, the wisdom-flowers-and-fruits of the buddhas cannot be accomplished. This places the *downward-transforming* dimension in **structural necessity** to the *upward-bearing* dimension — not merely a moral admonition to compassion but a constitutive condition for the realisation of bodhi itself. Vow nine's closure: *"my accommodating is without end ..."*[^14] Vow nine's verse-couplet: *"I always accommodate every sentient being, to the end of every kalpa to come ..."*[^15]

Vow nine's dimension is **downward-transforming**. Zongmi calls vow nine dedication to sentient beings; Chengguan, in his separate account of the three directions, says that dedication toward sentient beings relies on function. Read together, the two passages support a local relation between vow nine, sentient beings, and function. Chengguan does not independently present this as a vow-by-vow classification of vow nine. The *Vimalakīrti-nirdeśa*'s *Bodhisattva* chapter (T0475) supplies a one-line cross-reference.

### 3.4 Vow Ten: Universal Dedication — the Closure of Total Dedication ⭐

Vow ten's principal exposition opens immediately with the retrospective totalising sentence: *"as for universal dedication: from the initial reverencing through to the accommodating, all merits whatsoever are entirely dedicated to all sentient beings throughout the dharmadhātu, throughout space; may all sentient beings forever attain ease and peace, free of every illness and pain … may every evil deed they would do come to nothing, and every wholesome work they undertake be swiftly accomplished; may the gates of every evil destination be closed, and the upright path to the human, divine, and nirvāṇic states be opened wide."*[^16] The opening sentence — *"from the initial reverencing through to the accommodating, all merits whatsoever are entirely dedicated"* — is the textual core of vow ten: it gathers the merits of vows one through nine (from vow one's *reverencing* to vow nine's *accommodating*, through every intervening operator) and dedicates them to sentient beings and to the buddha-path. The mid-section of vow ten contains the **substituting-for-the-most-grievous-suffering passage**: *"if sentient beings, by reason of the karma they have piled up, undergo every kind of most grievous painful fruit, I shall undergo it in their stead."*[^17] This is the substantive other-benefitting face of dedication — the bodhisattva's bearing of others' suffering. Vow ten's closure: *"my dedicating is without end ..."*[^18] The thirty-second stanza of the verse-section is the seven-vow summary verse[^1], and the closing seal stanza of the entire fascicle — *"these supreme practices of Samantabhadra are mine ..."* — supplies vow ten's deathbed-vow ritual realisation.

⭐ Vow ten's interpretive spine is supplied jointly by Zongmi and Chengguan. Zongmi's *Detailed Sub-Commentary*, at X05.0266c06–07, articulates the three-location dedication schema:

> *"Vow eight is dedication to bodhi; vow nine is dedication to sentient beings; vow ten is the proper dedication, together comprising the three locations."*[^19]

This is the strongest sub-commentarial textual anchor for the present paper's thesis. Vow eight = *dedication to bodhi* (the upward-bearing direction); vow nine = *dedication to sentient beings* (the downward-transforming direction); vow ten = *the proper dedication* — *gathering both directions and adding the third direction toward the absolute (toward thusness, toward the dharmadhātu)*. The three-direction dedication schema therefore explicitly states the **closure-triad** structure that the present paper's thesis names. Zongmi further articulates the grammatical source of *above-bearing-and-below-transforming* through the line *"upward-uniting-with-the-buddhas' compassionate strength; downward-uniting-with-sentient-beings' lamenting plea."*[^20] Chengguan's *Practice-and-Vow Chapter Commentary* extends the schema by pairing the three directions with the three greats (*substance-attribute-function*):

> *"Toward the absolute, relying on substance; toward bodhi, relying on attribute; toward sentient beings, relying on function."*[^21]

Chengguan's line *"transforming below with great compassion, seeking above with great wisdom"* (大悲下化、大智上求) explains the downward and upward directions within the same discussion.[^22] Read together with Zongmi's vows-eight-through-ten alignment, these passages support the local three-direction interpretation. Their evidentiary reach stops there: they do not map vows one through seven or articulate the whole-ten-vow ordering proposed in §III.5.

A philological discipline must be marked here. The named-triple *"dedication-to-sentient-beings / dedication-to-bodhi / dedication-to-the-absolute"* is **a sub-commentary-stratum codification, not a direct sūtra-treatise canonical naming**. T1509 fascicle sixty-one's *Rejoicing-and-Dedication* chapter contains only the operative schema (the three marks, the three modes of reverence) and does not formally name the triple; the complete fifteen-record *Mahāprajñāpāramitā-sūtra* family (T05n0220a; T06n0220b; T07n0220c; T07n0220d; T07n0220e; T07n0220f; T07n0220g; T07n0220h; T07n0220i; T07n0220j; T07n0220k; T07n0220l; T07n0220m; T07n0220n; T07n0220o) likewise lacks the named triple. The complete codification of *"sentient beings / bodhi / absolute"* as a named-triple appears to consolidate at the Zhiyi–Fazang–Chengguan–Zongmi sub-commentary stratum. The present paper accordingly cites the named-triple strictly from X05n0227 (Chengguan) and X05n0229 (Zongmi), not from T1509 (this caveat is given its full middle-way articulation at §V). Likewise, the phrase *"emptiness of the threefold sphere"* (*三輪體空*) — agent of dedication, object of dedication, recipient of dedication, all three empty — does not appear in this exact wording within T1509 or that complete fifteen-record family, though its *substance* is canonical: T1509 articulates the same content through the **three marks** (designation-mark, dharma-mark, signless-mark). The present paper accordingly uses *"emptiness of the three marks"* (*三相空*, per T1509) as the audited replacement for *"emptiness of the threefold sphere,"* avoiding citation of a phrase whose precise textual locus has not been independently verified (the full caveat appears at §V).

### 3.5 Vows One through Ten: An Authorial Substance-Attribute-Function Heuristic

This subsection moves from the verifiable commentarial layer to an authorial hermeneutical proposal. Chengguan maps the three directions of dedication to substance, attribute, and function while commenting on vow ten; Zongmi separately aligns vows eight, nine, and ten with those three locations. Neither author configures vows one through seven. The following scheme is **Shi Huijing's retrospective heuristic for synthesising NIAN P26–P28**, not a scriptural taxonomy or a Chengguan–Zongmi teaching:

> **Authorial correspondence 1: vows 1–3 (reverencing, praising, offering) → *substance* (*tǐ* 體), as the opening three-karma assembly.**
> **Authorial correspondence 2: vows 4–9 (repentance, rejoicing, requesting, following, accommodating) → *attribute* (*xiàng* 相), as a range of practical modes.**
> **Authorial correspondence 3: vow 10 (universal dedication) → *function* (*yòng* 用), as the outward extension of the preceding merits.**

Five considerations motivate the heuristic. Each is an authorial analogy, not a name assigned to the vows by the primary sources.

*First*, the author maps **vows 1–3 to substance** because NIAN P26 reads reverencing, praising, and offering through body, speech, and mind. This opening three-karma assembly can be compared with the *Awakening of Faith*'s *"substance-greatness"*: *"all dharmas, true thusness, equal, neither increasing nor decreasing."*[^23] T1666 supplies the vocabulary of substance-greatness; it does not identify Samantabhadra's first three vows with that category. The connection is the present paper's interpretive choice.

*Second*, the author maps **vows 4–9 to attribute** because P27 treats repentance and rejoicing as two transformations of karma, P28 treats the two requests as modes of sustaining the dharma, and the present paper adds the upward and downward directions of vows eight and nine. These six practical modes may be compared with the *Awakening of Faith*'s *"attribute-greatness"*: *"the tathāgatagarbha fully equipped with immeasurable virtuous attributes."*[^23] T1666 does not establish this six-vow correspondence; the grouping is the author's organising device.

*Third*, the author maps **vow 10 to function** because its opening explicitly gathers the merits of vows one through nine and turns them toward all sentient beings. This outward operation can be compared with the *Awakening of Faith*'s *"function-greatness"*, which is *"able to give rise to all wholesome cause-and-fruit, mundane and supramundane."*[^23] The comparison must not erase a crucial difference: in Chengguan's three-direction passage, the direction toward the absolute relies on *substance*, not function. “Vow ten as function” is therefore the present paper's functional analogy, not Chengguan's classification.

*Fourth*, Chengguan's phrase *"transforming below with great compassion, seeking above with great wisdom"* describes the downward and upward directions of dedication.[^22] It does **not** encode the whole ten-vow set. The present paper uses that directional language for vows eight and nine, then independently reviews the preceding vows. The evidence supplied by Chengguan and the extension supplied by the author must remain distinct.

*Fifth*, the proposal's scholarly status must remain explicit: it is **Shi Huijing's modern authorial synthesis**. X0227 does not state where vows one through seven belong within substance, attribute, and function; X0229 likewise stops with the alignment of vows eight through ten and the three locations of dedication. This paper does not claim to know that the extension accords with Chengguan's unspoken intent. Its value must instead be judged by whether it preserves the wording and role of each vow, clarifies the relation among NIAN P26–P28, and consistently labels analogy as analogy. Other whole-ten-vow arrangements remain possible.[^24]

Paul Williams's *Mahāyāna Buddhism: The Doctrinal Foundations* supplies comparative English-language vocabulary for discussing Huayan and the three greats. Williams does not map substance, attribute, and function onto vows 1–3, 4–9, and 10; that correspondence belongs solely to this paper. Section IV therefore uses the mapping as a modern interpretive tool, not as historical evidence against Cook.

### 3.6 Chinese Ritual-Practice Closure: the Convergence of Three Traditions

The Chinese ritual codification of vow ten consolidates across three converging traditions.

**The repentance-liturgy tradition.** Zhiyi's *Lotus Samādhi Repentance Liturgy* (T1941) sets out a ten-section curriculum: (1) purifying the ritual platform, (2) purifying the body, (3) three-karma offering, (4) inviting the buddhas, (5) praising, (6) reverencing the buddhas, (7) **clarifying-the-method-of-dedication and clarifying-the-method-of-vow-making**, (8) reciting the *sūtra*, (9) seated meditation, (10) seated meditation upon the true mark. **The corrected placement is the seventh section, not the tenth**: an earlier mis-placement (correctable at the level of the original *kepan* division) had located *dedication-and-vow* at the tenth section; the original division places *seated meditation upon the true mark* at the tenth and *dedication-and-vow* at the seventh.[^5] Zhiyi's seventh-section codification is the **mother-text of Chinese repentance-liturgy dedication**; all subsequent Tiantai-line repentance liturgies derive their dedication ritual articulation from this seven-section template.

**The reverence-and-praise tradition.** Shandao's *Verses for the Six Times of Reverence-Praise* (T1980) instantiates the **five mind-gates**: reverencing, praising, vow-making, contemplating, **dedicating**. The fifth dedication-gate's textual anchor *"to the limits of space and dharmadhātu, my vows are likewise so"* fully echoes vow ten's *"throughout the dharmadhātu, throughout space, all sentient beings"* universalising scope. Shandao's five-mind-gate structure was developed at length in NIAN P26; here only the fifth dedication-gate's ritual articulation is referred to by single-line cross-reference.

**The buddha-recitation tradition.** Yongming Yanshou's *Universal Convergence of the Ten Thousand Goods* (T2017) articulates the substance-function frame through the line *"Samantabhadra adorns principle through practice"* — Samantabhadra's myriad practices are the *phenomenal-attribute* face, the buddhas and the dharma are the *principle-substance* face, and *practice-adorning-principle* is the substance-and-function unity. Yunqi Zhuhong's *Sub-Commentary on the Amitābha Sūtra* (X22n0424) articulates *"Samantabhadra's vow-king is not apart from the present moment of mind"*[^25] — the ten-vow set is condensed and gathered in the present moment of buddha-recitation. Ouyi Zhixu's *Essentials of the Amitābha Sūtra* (T1762) articulates *"the compass of the ten thousand practices does not exceed this"* — Samantabhadra's myriad practices are all gathered into the faith-vow-recitation framework. The *Three-Time Mindfulness Service* (X74n1464, traditionally attributed to the Zhongfeng line) chants the full ten-vow set within its three-recitation ritual structure. The convergence forms the strongest Chinese-tradition fused-anchor:

> Yunqi Zhuhong's *Sub-Commentary on the Amitābha Sūtra*: *"Samantabhadra's vow-king is not apart from the present moment of mind."*
> + Shandao's *Verses for the Six Times of Reverence-Praise*: the universalising space-and-dharmadhātu echo of the fifth dedication-gate.
> + Zhiyi's *Lotus Samādhi Repentance Liturgy*: the seventh-section *clarifying-the-method-of-dedication*.

⚠️ A directional caveat must be marked, carried over from NIAN P28 §V. The Pure Land system's **dedication-toward-rebirth-in-the-Pure-Land** and Samantabhadra's **universal dedication** are **directionally inverse**: the former is *sentient-being → Pure-Land* (going-to-be-reborn-there); the latter is *one's own merit → all sentient beings and the buddha-path* (extending to all). Yunqi's and Ouyi's gathering of Samantabhadra's myriad practices into buddha-recitation **does not amount to claiming the two are identical** — what is gathered is the *interiorising dimension* of vow ten (the *not-apart-from-the-present-moment-of-mind* dimension), not the conflation of Samantabhadra's outward universalising direction with the Pure Land system's interiorising-to-rebirth direction. This caveat preserves the directional integrity of both invitation cosmologies; conflation undoes both. The point receives its full middle-way articulation at §V.

## IV. Anglophone Interlocutors: A Critical Reassessment of Cook 1977

This section places the paper's practice-oriented proposal beside Francis H. Cook's *Hua-yen Buddhism: The Jewel Net of Indra* (1977), then uses Williams 2009 and Hamar 2007 as comparative interlocutors. Because the whole-ten-vow mapping is an authorial heuristic, it cannot serve as proof that the tradition itself rejected Cook's reading.

### 4.1 Cook 1977: A Critical Reassessment of the Metaphysics-of-Totality Reading ★

Francis H. Cook's *Hua-yen Buddhism: The Jewel Net of Indra* (Pennsylvania State University Press, 1977) is among the foundational monographs of Anglophone Huayan studies. Cook's central thesis: Huayan = **metaphysics-of-totality** — the *dharmadhātu* understood as the perfectly mutually-interpenetrating **jewel-net of Indra**. Chapter eight, *"Living in the Net of Indra,"* is the principal locus of Cook's reading of the ten great vows: the vows are read as **exemplifications** (*exemplifications*) of the metaphysics — concrete instantiations of *dharmadhātu* totality on the practice side. Chapters four (*Identity*), five (*Intercausality*), and six (*Part and Whole*) supply the metaphysical apparatus.

The present paper's pushback against Cook's reading is set out along three converging lines.

*The first line is a practice-oriented alternative.* T0293 directly supports the common closure pattern and vow ten's gathering of the first nine. On that basis, Shi Huijing heuristically maps vows 1–3, 4–9, and 10 onto substance, attribute, and function. The mapping can draw attention to the vows' ritual and practical operations, but it is a modern authorial model. It cannot be presented as ancient evidence that Cook's reading is invalid. The more modest claim is that a practice-oriented reading deserves to stand beside a metaphysics-of-totality reading.

*The second line is a historical-philological limit.* Chengguan attests that the *Practice-and-Vow Chapter* is absent from T0278 and T0279, while the complete ten-vow sequence appears in the late-eighth-century T0293. This chronology requires careful distinction among the earlier recensions, Fazang's thought, and the later ten-vow unit. It does not by itself prove that the vows admit only a ritual reading, nor does it alone refute Cook. What it establishes is the date and recensional scope within which historical claims must remain.

*The third line is the convergence of the Chinese ritual traditions.* As §III.6 has set out, the Chinese codification of vow ten consolidates across three independent traditions: the repentance-liturgy tradition (Zhiyi, T1941, seventh section); the reverence-and-praise tradition (Shandao, T1980, fifth gate); the buddha-recitation tradition (Yongming, T2017; Yunqi, X22n0424; Ouyi, T1762; the Zhongfeng-attributed *Three-Time Mindfulness Service*, X74n1464). The character of this triple convergence is **ritual-operative articulation**, not metaphysical exemplification. The eighth-to-twelfth-century Chinese reading of Samantabhadra's vow-king is, on the practice side, a codification of ritual operations — not the illustration of a metaphysical totality.

A concession is required. Cook's *dharmadhātu* framework has textual and intellectual-historical grounds within Huayan. Metaphysical and ritual-practical readings need not exclude one another. The present paper therefore offers a practice-oriented supplement rather than claiming that its authorial substance-attribute-function heuristic establishes the vows' single correct historical meaning.

### 4.2 Williams 2009: Substance-Attribute-Function Framework, Conceded-and-Extended

Paul Williams's *Mahāyāna Buddhism: The Doctrinal Foundations* (Routledge, 2nd ed. 2009) provides comparative English-language vocabulary for Huayan and tathāgatagarbha thought. The present paper borrows the terms *substance*, *attribute*, and *function*, then independently maps them onto vows 1–3, 4–9, and 10. Williams does not make that mapping; his work supplies comparative vocabulary, not source proof for the paper's whole-ten-vow heuristic.

### 4.3 Hamar 2007: Sudhana Macro-Frame, Conceded-and-Extended

Imre Hamar's edited volume *Reflecting Mirrors: Perspectives on Huayan Buddhism* (Harrassowitz Verlag, 2007) provides multiple perspectives on Huayan. This paper places Sudhana's pilgrimage ending with Samantabhadra beside vow ten's gathering of merits “from the initial reverencing through to the accommodating.” The resulting comparison between narrative culmination and ritual gathering is the present paper's heuristic analogy. It is not a whole-ten-vow substance-attribute-function mapping stated by T0293, Chengguan, Zongmi, or Hamar.

### 4.4 Faure 1991 Held to Background

Bernard Faure's *The Rhetoric of Immediacy* (Princeton University Press, 1991), the principal pushback ally for *performative cosmology* in NIAN P28, is here held to background. Faure's framework remains the implicit ground of §IV — vow ten's universal-dedication ritual is a **performative-cosmological field-construction**: the dedication's verbal-and-bodily act *itself constructs* the cosmological field within which the *universal* of *universal dedication* operates. McMahan 2002 *Empty Vision*'s visionary-imagery reading is gestured at within §VI's cultivation guidance as a single-line cameo and is not engaged at §IV. Cleary's three-volume *Flower Ornament Scripture* (Shambhala, 1984–1987) is the primary English translation reference and appears in the bibliography.

## V. Middle-Way Corrections

This section, following the *Pointing at the Moon* methodology's two-marker convention (❌ for misreadings to be rejected, ⚠️ for cross-disciplinary or philological caveats), addresses five corrections specific to the closure-triad of vows eight, nine, and ten. Earlier middle-way corrections (P26's vow-king-totality-and-not-pantheism, P27's repentance-and-not-self-punishment, P28's request-as-not-theistic-petition and as-not-modernist-psychologising) are referred to only by single-line cross-reference where vow ten requires continuity.

❌ **Misreading 1: Reading vow ten's *universal dedication* as *merit-as-economic-transaction*.** This misreading — the commodification pole — treats bodhisattva merit as a transferable commodity and *dedication* as the transactional gesture of moving merit from one ledger to another. The error: dedication's grammatical core (*pariṇāmana*, *turning-toward, inclining-toward*) is **a ritual-cosmological articulation**, not an economic transfer. Its character is the *turning-toward of the emptiness of the three marks* (per T1509 fascicle sixty-one's three marks: designation-mark, dharma-mark, signless-mark); when the three marks are without distinction, no *transferable commodity* is constituted in the first place. The commodification reading reduces dedication to economic transfer, draining it of its ritual-cosmological articulation. Yunqi Zhuhong's *"Samantabhadra's vow-king is not apart from the present moment of mind"* is the Chinese-tradition articulation of this middle-way correction: when, within the present moment of mind, there is no distinction-among-the-three-marks (no *agent of dedication, commodity dedicated, recipient of dedication*), then *universal* dedication operates in its proper register.

❌ **Misreading 2: Reducing vow ten to *purely interior mindful presencing*.** This misreading — the modernist-psychologising pole — reduces the entire vow-king to an inward training of mindful attention, draining the *universal* (*pǔ-jiē* 普皆) dimension altogether. The error: *universal dedication*'s *universal* is the explicit cosmological universalising scope-marker — *throughout the dharmadhātu, throughout space, all sentient beings* — not assimilable to *inward-only* attention. Yunqi's and Ouyi's gathering of Samantabhadra's myriad practices into buddha-recitation **does not** advance the claim that vow ten is purely interior mindfulness; their reading is that the *universal-outward-architecture* is *condensed-and-gathered* in the present moment of buddha-recitation — the outer is contained-in-the-inner, the inner manifesting the outer. Misreadings 1 and 2 are the two extremes between which the middle-way reading must thread: the first over-objectifies (dedication-as-commodity); the second over-interiorises (dedication-as-inner-attention-only). The middle-way reading is **outward ritual-cosmological articulation** — neither transfer of objects nor purely inner mindfulness.

⚠️ **Hermeneutical boundary.** T0278 and T0279 do not contain the *Practice-and-Vow Chapter*, while the complete Chinese ten-vow sequence appears in T0293. X0227 explains the three directions of dedication while commenting on vow ten; X0229 explicitly aligns only vows eight through ten with the three locations. Neither text assigns vows one through seven to substance, attribute, and function. The 1–3 / 4–9 / 10 arrangement must therefore be labelled Shi Huijing's authorial synthesis, not an early Huayan consensus or a late-Tang commentarial doctrine.

⚠️ **Directional discrimination: Pure Land *dedication-toward-rebirth* and Samantabhadra's *universal dedication* are directionally inverse.** The two are members of the same family of dedication-cosmologies but **structurally inverse** — the former is *sentient-being → Pure-Land* (going-to-be-reborn-there); the latter is *one's own merit → all sentient beings and the buddha-path* (extending to all). Yunqi's and Ouyi's gathering of Samantabhadra's myriad practices into buddha-recitation **does not** assert an identity between the two; their reading interiorises the *not-apart-from-the-present-moment-of-mind* dimension of vow ten without conflating its outward universalising direction with the Pure Land's inward-bound direction. This caveat is carried over from NIAN P28 §V and re-articulated here so that the cross-tradition gathering of patriarchal lines is not misread as a directional unification of two inverse dedication-cosmologies.

⚠️ **Vocabulary discipline for the named-triple *and* the *threefold-sphere-emptiness* phrase.** Two related caveats are gathered here. *First*: the named-triple *"dedication-to-sentient-beings / dedication-to-bodhi / dedication-to-the-absolute"* is a sub-commentary-stratum codification, not a sūtra-treatise canonical naming. T1509 *Mahāprajñāpāramitā-śāstra* fascicle sixty-one and the complete fifteen-record *Mahāprajñāpāramitā-sūtra* family named in §III.4 contain only operative schemas (the three marks, the three modes of reverence) without the named triple. The complete codification appears to consolidate in the Zhiyi–Fazang–Chengguan–Zongmi sub-commentary line. The present paper accordingly cites the named-triple from X05n0227 (Chengguan) and X05n0229 (Zongmi) only, **not** from T1509. *Second*: the precise phrase *"emptiness of the threefold sphere"* (*三輪體空*) — agent of dedication, object of dedication, recipient of dedication, all three empty — does not appear in this exact wording within T1509 or those fifteen records; the substantive content is canonical (T1509 articulates the same content through the three marks: designation-mark, dharma-mark, signless-mark), but the verbal formula's complete consolidation appears to be later. The present paper accordingly uses *"emptiness of the three marks"* (*三相空*, per T1509) as the audited replacement, ensuring that the philological locus of every cited phrase is verifiable. This vocabulary discipline allows the paper's citations to be fully auditable.

## VI. Conclusion

### Essence Restated

Samantabhadra's vows eight (*constantly following the Buddha in study*), nine (*accommodating sentient beings*), and ten (*universal dedication*) compose the **above-bearing-and-below-transforming dedication closure-triad**: vow eight is the upward-bearing dimension (seeking enlightenment, depending on attribute, *prajñā*); vow nine is the downward-transforming dimension (transforming sentient beings, depending on function, *vimukti*); vow ten is the total dedication (toward the absolute, embracing both, depending on substance, *dharmakāya*). The closure-formula isomorphism is now confirmed for the fourth time across vows one through ten, 100% across the entire ten-vow set, with only the operator verb varying. Vow ten's totalising function is *not* encoded in the closure layer — vow ten's closure structure is itself isomorphic with that of vows one through nine; the totalising function is encoded, instead, in vow ten's opening retrospective sentence: *"from the initial reverencing through to the accommodating, all merits whatsoever are entirely dedicated."*

Beyond that source-supported local structure, the paper proposes an **authorial retrospective heuristic**: vows 1–3 correspond to *substance*, vows 4–9 to *attribute*, and vow 10 to *function*. Zongmi's vows-eight-through-ten alignment and Chengguan's three-direction passage motivate the comparison but do not state the whole-ten-vow map. Chengguan's phrase *"transforming below with great compassion, seeking above with great wisdom"* describes two directions of dedication; it does not encode vows one through ten.

The conclusion therefore preserves three levels. *First*, T0293's common closure pattern and vow ten's opening establish textual continuity and the gathering of the preceding nine vows. *Second*, Zongmi links vows eight through ten to the three locations, while Chengguan maps the three directions of dedication to substance, attribute, and function. *Third*, Shi Huijing's 1–3 / 4–9 / 10 mapping is a modern heuristic for organising the series. It carries no claim to Chengguan's intention and no canonical or sectarian authority; “vow ten as function” is specifically the paper's outward-functional analogy, not a replacement for Chengguan's “toward the absolute, relying on substance.”

### Cultivation Guidance

For modern Anglophone practitioners, the **closing dedicatory cluster of T0293 fascicle forty** — the thirty-two-stanza verse-section, opening with *"that I may purify the practice of Samantabhadra in every place"* and reaching, via *"when the time of my death is at hand,"* to *"I, having been reborn in that buddha-realm"* and culminating in the closing seal *"swiftly proceed to the buddha-realm of Infinite Light"* — supplies the principal practice-source. The cluster is the concrete ritual form of vow ten's deathbed-vow ritual realisation: *the deathbed vow* and *universal dedication* are gathered into a single ritual articulation. The closing seal stanza is taken here as the paper's closing frame device:

> *"These supreme practices of Samantabhadra are mine,
> and the boundless surpassing merits — every one of them — are dedicated.
> May all sentient beings drowning in saṃsāra
> swiftly reach the buddha-realm of Infinite Light."*[^2]

The paper's first-and-last frame is now closed: the opening epigraph is the thirty-second stanza (the seven-vow summary verse), framing the ten-vow set's totalising verse-anchor; the closing epigraph is the closing seal stanza (the deathbed-vow ritual realisation of vow ten). Read together, the two stanzas frame Part VII's closer paper.

### First-and-Last Echo to NIAN P25

Within the same frame, the source-level answer to P25's question—*what gathers the vow-king as a whole?*—is vow ten's explicit dedication of all merits from the first reverencing through the ninth vow's accommodation. This paper adds the substance-attribute-function mapping as one way of reading Part VII's five papers together. That authorial map is neither the vow-king's only possible meaning nor a self-description found in the scripture.

### Open Questions

Two open questions are offered to the reader as forward-references.

**Open Question 1.** Can this paper's authorial substance-attribute-function heuristic illuminate comparison with other Mahāyāna vow structures? Any such comparison would have to begin afresh from each source; recurrence of the same pattern cannot be presumed.

**Open Question 2.** If vow ten is compared heuristically with function-greatness, how can universal dedication be practised without becoming a model of merit as economic transfer? This too tests the author's analogy; it is not a classification already supplied by the scripture.

The paper rests here. **T0293 explicitly gathers the first nine vows within universal dedication; Zongmi and Chengguan explicitly address the three locations and vows eight through ten. The 1–3 / 4–9 / 10 substance-attribute-function map is Shi Huijing's heuristic proposal, offered for examination and never as scripture or inherited commentarial fact.**

The three greats: *"first, *substance-greatness* — that is, all dharmas, true thusness, equal, neither increasing nor decreasing; second, *attribute-greatness* — that is, the *tathāgatagarbha* fully equipped with immeasurable virtuous attributes; third, *function-greatness* — able to give rise to all wholesome cause-and-fruit, mundane and supramundane."* This passage is the locus classicus of the *substance-attribute-function* schema; the philological dispute over the text's authorship is held to footnote 4.

[^24]: The 1–3 / 4–9 / 10 cross-mapping is Shi Huijing's authorial synthesis of NIAN P26–P28. Chengguan's X0227 sentence occurs while explaining vow ten's dedication of cultivated good toward three locations: *"toward the absolute, relying on substance; toward bodhi, relying on attribute; toward sentient beings, relying on function"* (X05.0195b03–04). Zongmi separately aligns vows eight, nine, and ten with dedication to bodhi, dedication to sentient beings, and the dedication comprising all three locations. Neither source assigns vows one through seven to substance, attribute, and function. The paper therefore makes no claim about Chengguan's unspoken intent; its whole-ten-vow map remains a modern heuristic.

[^25]: Yunqi Zhuhong, *Sub-Commentary on the Amitābha Sūtra*, X22n0424. The line *"Samantabhadra's vow-king is not apart from the present moment of mind"* (*普賢願王不離當念*) is the most concentrated Chinese-patriarchal articulation of the gathering of the ten-vow set into the present moment of buddha-recitation. The line is the textual basis of §III.6's buddha-recitation tradition closure and is the Chinese-tradition middle-way correction of misreading 1 (commodification) at §V.
